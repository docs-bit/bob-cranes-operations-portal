# Industrial Theme Reference Notes

## Verified reference findings: crops 1–2

- The top visual system uses a near-black navy base with deep blue angular overlays, supported by construction yellow, safety orange, and white as the principal contrast colors.
- Primary headings are condensed, uppercase, and high contrast. Yellow is used selectively to emphasize important words rather than as a general text color.
- The hero combines a dark operational surface, an equipment focal visual, clipped/angled panel edges, orange action buttons, compact metric tiles, and yellow information cards.
- Light sections introduce white and pale gray surfaces but retain black headline typography, angular yellow callouts, disciplined spacing, and dark brand bands.
- For the portal, the appropriate translation is an industrial control system rather than a marketing-page replica: dark navigation and command surfaces; yellow status emphasis; orange action emphasis; off-white work areas; and clearly accessible text/icon states.

## Verified reference findings: crops 3–4

- Repeated card families use spacious pale-gray panels, short black headings, yellow action strips, and clipped lower-right corners. Their hierarchy depends on strong grouping, not decorative density.
- Alternating light and near-black sections create a clear operational rhythm. The darker feature panel uses a large yellow equipment image, a small orange annotation card, and an orange call-to-action to focus attention.
- Compact orange and yellow elements carry specific intent. In the portal, that maps to primary actions and critical operational cues, while neutral cards should remain restrained.
- Service tiles show a scalable pattern for workspace modules: a high-contrast icon block, uppercase label, descriptive detail, and a direct action—suitable for department and compliance cards.

## Verified reference findings: crops 5–6

- Editorial equipment photography is deployed as a constrained operational highlight, with a navy information panel over or adjacent to imagery. Portal use should remain decorative and never obscure booking or safety information.
- Process cards alternate yellow and navy and use large step numerals. This is a strong fit for the existing eight-stage booking lifecycle, provided lifecycle status remains readable to every role.
- Angle cuts are most effective as occasional section dividers and card corners; they should not be applied indiscriminately to data tables or dense forms where rectangular scan lines improve usability.

## Verified reference findings: crops 7–8

- Dark editorial bands pair white cards and muted imagery with clipped-card accents. The portal can use this motif for summary sections and activity feeds without fabricating client testimonials or ratings.
- The reference uses a clear long-page cadence: dark highlight, light work surface, then dark highlight. Within the portal, this translates to a permanent deep-navy navigation shell with readable off-white task surfaces and selected dark feature modules.
- Thumbnail-led rows support a compact information hierarchy. The operations equivalent is status-led rows with colored chips, owner context, and time, rather than decorative imagery in dense audit and booking tables.

## Verified reference findings: crops 9–10

- Light work sections use bold type, thin dividers, and disciplined FAQ/form spacing. The portal should use these patterns for filters, inputs, and expandable record details rather than applying visual effects to every control.
- Final conversion blocks return to deep navy with an orange action panel. The portal equivalent is a distinct primary command treatment for actions such as New booking, Submit for review, and Dispatch.
- The full reference supports a theme defined by navy #05091d–#0a1230, construction yellow #ffc400, safety orange #f36b13, warm white #f8f7f3, charcoal #151515, crisp white text, strong contrast, compact uppercase labels, and purposeful clipped-corner accents.

## Implementation verification

The authenticated Operations Cockpit was reviewed after the shared theme layer loaded. The live view shows a navy command bar and navigation shell, yellow selected-navigation and progress markers, orange primary action treatment, off-white work canvas, uppercase condensed headings, and a dark booking-progress module. Booking records, filters, lifecycle stages, and attendance controls remain visible and reachable in the restyled view.
